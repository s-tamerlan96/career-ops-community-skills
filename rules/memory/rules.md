# Process rules

This file contains generic workflow controls. Store candidate facts in the approved user-layer sources, not here. Add future durable controls only through a confirmed review.

## Evidence and scope

- Distinguish facts from interpretation, hypotheses, and missing evidence. Include a source and date when they affect a decision.
- Match the claim to the evidence: reported experience, observed interview dialogue, employer feedback, public research, and mock practice have different limits.
- Preserve confirmed responsibilities, collaboration, decision rights, and authorship boundaries. Using a tool or contributing to a project does not establish sole ownership or authorship.
- Treat ambiguous dictation as ambiguous. Clarify a fragment before turning it into a new achievement, domain, project, or gap.
- For post-hire work, establish confirmed scope and dependencies before proposing targets, timelines, or promotion claims. A suggested plan is not an agreed manager expectation.

## Case continuity and closure

- Read the case manifest, handoff, and their required evidence before resuming. Check that references exist and that dates, stages, and communication status agree.
- Separate prepared, reviewed, authorized, sent, and acknowledged communication. Do not infer delivery from the existence of a draft.
- When an outcome is confirmed, update the existing opportunity status, case log, and retrospective as appropriate. Record what changed, the source, and the next action; avoid duplicate tracker entries.
- A rejection or offer confirms the outcome, not its cause. Keep possible explanations separate unless the source establishes them.
- Keep employer-specific learning with the case. Transfer reusable learning into a question or story bank only with appropriate evidence and privacy boundaries.

## Professional feedback

- Read the literal wording first, then consider courtesy, softened criticism, selective disclosure, or a positive-sandwich structure.
- Rank explicit decisions and actions above behavior-specific feedback, concrete development gaps, and generic warmth.
- Friendly tone does not establish a positive hiring outcome. Specific praise can support the named competency without proving the overall assessment.
- Do not reinterpret every positive statement as a hidden negative. Present the literal evidence, plausible interpretation, and unknowns separately.
- Recalibrate earlier interpretations when a concrete next step or outcome becomes known.

## Interview work

- Ground preparation in the actual role, interviewer context when known, and the requested interview language. Cover likely opening questions before specialized cases.
- Reconstruct who asked what before evaluating an answer. Separate an initial question, follow-up, candidate answer, and candidate's reverse question.
- A high-level recap cannot establish exact wording, timing, delivery, pressure, or interviewer reaction. Limit conclusions to the evidence available.
- Assess substance before polishing language. Show the actual answer, a stronger answer pattern, and the difference; preserve truthful scope and the candidate's voice.
- Do not treat a sample stronger answer as something the candidate actually said or did.

## Privacy

- Keep private conversations, compensation details, identity data, and employer material in the user-approved storage and access boundary.
- Public reusable examples must be synthetic or specifically cleared for publication. Changing a name alone does not anonymize a distinctive real case.
- Do not publish private source links, transcript fragments, local identity paths, histories, or credentials with a reusable skill package.

# PM Interview Rubric

Use this rubric to diagnose what an answer demonstrates at the target interview stage. Score only dimensions the question actually tests.

## Score Scale

### 1: weak

- no relevant example or decision;
- generic theory;
- unclear personal role;
- unsupported impact;
- no reflection where reflection is expected.

### 2: below bar

- relevant situation but shallow ownership;
- chronology replaces reasoning;
- material constraints or trade-offs are absent;
- scope is below the target level;
- evidence cannot survive a follow-up.

### 3: credible

- direct answer with a relevant example;
- responsibility and main actions are understandable;
- reasoning is plausible;
- result is observable or honestly bounded;
- depth or seniority is only partly demonstrated.

### 4: strong

- clear personal ownership;
- structured diagnosis;
- meaningful alternatives and trade-offs;
- stakeholder and execution complexity;
- credible measurement;
- nuanced reflection;
- answer increases confidence for the target role.

### 5: exceptional

- judgment matches the target scope;
- candidate changes a system, operating model, or decision quality;
- conflicting incentives and second-order effects are handled;
- result and causal limits are explicit;
- mistakes and limits are explained without defensiveness;
- follow-ups strengthen rather than weaken the example.

Do not average mechanically. A critical dimension can be a hiring blocker even when the overall answer is polished.

## Core Dimensions

### Directness and structure

Can the interviewer understand the answer, decision, and result without reconstructing them?

### Relevance

Does the answer address the actual question, stage, and target role?

### Personal ownership

Is it clear what the candidate personally diagnosed, decided, changed, delegated, or escalated?

### Scope and seniority

Does the example demonstrate the scale, ambiguity, and decision authority expected at the target level?

### Product judgment

Does the candidate connect customer behavior, business value, constraints, product mechanics, and opportunity cost?

### Diagnosis

Does the candidate distinguish signals, hypotheses, evidence gaps, root causes, and chosen analysis?

### Options and trade-offs

Were meaningful alternatives considered? Is the decision rule visible?

### Execution

Can the candidate turn ambiguity into coordinated action, manage dependencies, and control risk?

### Stakeholder leadership

Can the candidate work through conflicting incentives, decision rights, and influence without authority?

### Evidence and metrics

Are outcomes observable, definitions precise, guardrails relevant, and causal claims appropriately bounded?

### Reflection

Does the candidate name what was wrong, what changed afterward, and how later behavior differed?

### Spoken authenticity

Does the answer sound like something the candidate could actually say and defend?

## Stage Calibration

### HR or recruiter

Prioritize relevance, clarity, motivation, seniority fit, compensation, location, timeline, and risk control. Do not demand a deep case answer.

### Hiring manager

Prioritize ownership, judgment, scope, stakeholder behavior, repeatable operating principles, and credible proof.

### Product or domain round

Prioritize diagnosis, product mechanics, prioritization, metrics, experiment quality, implementation constraints, and the next decision.

### Analytics round

Prioritize metric definition, denominator and unit, cohort or segment logic, data sufficiency, hypothesis order, experiment design, guardrails, interpretation, and post-test decisions.

### Leadership or CPO round

Prioritize strategic choices, organizational leverage, decision rights, commercial judgment, executive communication, and self-awareness.

### Case interview

Prioritize clarification, problem framing, assumptions, alternatives, decision logic, measurement, risk, and iteration. Do not score framework name-dropping.

## Common Red Flags

- "We" hides personal responsibility.
- A team result is presented as an individual achievement.
- A target is presented as an achieved result.
- Outputs are presented as outcomes.
- Strategy is described as a feature list or roadmap.
- Analytics is treated as the product problem rather than a way to test hypotheses.
- Metrics have no definition, denominator, window, or decision use.
- Every conflict ends with the candidate being completely right.
- Reflection is a disguised strength.
- The answer is polished but collapses under factual follow-up.
- The candidate reveals private constraints or confidential data unnecessarily.
- Terminology is correct but no actual choice follows from it.

## Overall Score Out of 10

Translate the hiring read, not a numerical average:

- `1-2`: actively damaging;
- `3-4`: below the likely bar;
- `5-6`: credible but not differentiated;
- `7-8`: strong and interview-ready with limited fixes;
- `9`: exceptional for the target stage;
- `10`: reserve for an answer that is both excellent and fully defensible.

State which dimensions drove the score and which were not observable.
